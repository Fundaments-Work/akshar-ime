## Context

AksharIME is at **81.40%** native top-1 (AK-Freq, single-reference, full engine)
with a decoder top-50 oracle of **94.40%**. The existing roadmap
(`2026-09-05-roadmap-to-90.md`, same directory) has executed S1/S2/S4/S5 and left S3
(a lattice CRF, half-built in the working tree) as the only open lever, with an
honest self-estimate of 86–89 and "90 requires S3 as well."

That estimate is pessimistic for a specific reason: **the ranking stage — where
12.7 of the 18.6 missing points live — has been trained on 9,155 examples and
then deliberately discounted to 30% influence (γ=0.3).** Meanwhile 3.59M
annotation pairs and 86.1M corpus tokens sit unused by any discriminative
objective. The gap is not a missing model family; it is four orders of magnitude
of missing ranking supervision, plus a prior that is undefined for the words the
model gets wrong most.

This plan proposes four levers, none of which appear in the current roadmap,
ordered by (expected points)/(effort). It stays entirely classical (no neural
nets, ms latency, WASM-shippable) per the confirmed constraint, and reports
AK-Freq single-ref top-1 as the driver with multi-ref and `evaluate_context`
sentence accuracy tracked every cycle.

**Decisions taken:** benchmark leads, all three metrics reported; classical only;
overnight compute is available.

## The arithmetic this plan is built on

Decomposition of the 18.60 missing points, from `analyze_errors`:

$$
\underbrace{94.40 - 81.40 = 13.00}_{\text{ranking: gold generated, ranked below}} \;+\; \underbrace{100 - 94.40 = 5.60}_{\text{generation: gold never in top-50}}
$$

And the error taxonomy (measured at the 75% era; **W0 re-measures it**): 52% of
top-1 misses are *matra-only* — prediction and gold agree after stripping
vowel-length/nasal signs. If that ratio still holds:

$$
\text{skeleton accuracy} \;\approx\; 81.40 + 0.52 \times 18.60 \;=\; \mathbf{91.1\%}
$$

**The consonant skeleton is already right ~91% of the time.** A model that
resolved matra assignment perfectly, changing nothing else, would score 91.1.
That single number is why the plan's centrepiece (W4) is a factored matra model
rather than another whole-word ranker — and why 90 is reachable without
abandoning the classical stack.

## W0 — Measurement gate (cheap, run first)

Everything below is sized against numbers that were last measured at 75–78%
accuracy. Re-measure before spending compute.

**Re-run `src/bin/analyze_errors.rs`** against the *current* shipped chain
(`translit_model.bin` + discriminative reranker + bigram context). It already reports oracle
curves (`KS = [1,2,3,5,8,10,20,50]`), strict-vs-multi-ref top-1, and the
matra/halant/substantive taxonomy (`classify`, `strip_matching` at
`src/bin/analyze_errors.rs:311-336`). Two fixes needed: it currently hardcodes
`beam_width: 64` and `data/aksharantar/nep_test.json`, both stale — point it at
`test_devanagari.jsonl` at beam 256.

**Add one estimator to the same binary — the collision bound.** Build
$R \mapsto \{(D, f(D))\}$ over train ∪ valid ∪ test, with $f$ = corpus frequency
from `word_freq_text.bin`. Then

$$
\mathrm{Acc}^{*} \;=\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathbb{1}\!\left[D_i^{\text{gold}} = \arg\max_{D \in A(R_i)} f(D)\right]
$$

is the accuracy of a system with *perfect generation and a perfect frequency
prior* — no function of the roman string alone plus a unigram prior can beat it.
It is a hard reference point for the isolated-word benchmark. `build_refsets`
(`analyze_errors.rs:70-78`) already builds $A(R)$ from the train split; extend it
to weight by frequency and to include valid/test.

**Gate:** if the matra share has collapsed since the 75% era, W4's expected value
drops and W1/W3 carry the program. If $\mathrm{Acc}^{*}$ lands below ~92, say so
plainly in the docs — it reframes what "90 on this benchmark" can mean.

## W0 RESULTS (measured 2026-09-05, `analyze_errors`, beam 256, lm 0.85, vocab 0.75)

The gate has fired. Every number the plan was sized against is confirmed, and two
of them are sharper than assumed.

### The collision bound is not the problem

```
cases 4101  unambiguous 4037 (98.4%)  ambiguous 64 (1.6%)
collision bound Acc* = 99.66%
```

Only **1.6%** of test romans have any alternative native attested anywhere in
train ∪ valid ∪ test, and the frequency prior picks gold in most of those. **The
isolated-word benchmark has essentially no intrinsic ambiguity ceiling: 90 is not
blocked by irreducible collisions.** The mathematics doc's कल/काल argument is
sound in principle but rarely realised in this test set — the gold is almost
always the only attested reading of its roman string.

*Honest caveat:* A(R) here is built from *attested* Aksharantar pairs. A genuinely
ambiguous roman may appear once by sampling luck. The stronger form of this bound
is A(R) = lattice ∩ word-trie (`decode_in_words`, already implemented) — worth
running, but the W4 forensics below already answer the question it was asked for.

### The taxonomy holds, and W4's arithmetic is confirmed

| Bucket | n | engine top-1 | matra-only share of misses | **top-1 if matra solved** | oracle k=50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **AK-Freq** | 2108 | 81.55% | **50.4%** | **90.84%** | 94.40% |
| NE | 1993 | 38.64% | 35.3% | 60.31% | 74.80% |
| ALL | 4101 | 60.69% | 39.0% | 76.01% | 84.88% |

The plan predicted 91.1 from the 75%-era 52% matra share. Measured today: share
50.4%, **landing point 90.84%**. The centrepiece survives contact with the data.

Note `halant-only = 0` in every bucket — conjunct errors are not a separate class;
they fold into substantive. The taxonomy is really two classes, not three.

### The matra class is mostly winnable — and it indicts the prior, not the model

Forensics over the 196 AK-Freq matra-only engine misses:

| Sub-class | count | share | the lever that wins it |
|---|---|---|---|
| gold already in the engine's top-10 | 137 | **69.9%** | **W1** — pure ranking loss |
| gold is the *more frequent* word | 61 | 31.1% | **W1** — the prior was right, the ranker ignored it |
| gold is **OOV** in the 470k vocab | 22 | 11.2% | **W2** — the frequency cliff |
| **both** candidates OOV (no signal) | 52 | **26.5%** | **W2/W4** — shape and morphology only |
| prediction is the more frequent word | 61 | 31.1% | W5 context, or morphology |

Two conclusions change the ordering:

1. **69.9% of matra misses are ranking losses** — the gold was generated and sat in
   the top-10. W1 (jackknifed reranking) attacks the matra class directly, and does
   not need W4 to do it.
2. **37.7% of the matra class involves an OOV gold**, and the examples show exactly
   why — they are *inflected and compound* forms whose stems are common:

```
roman              gold:freq            predicted:freq
brahmanko          ब्राह्माणको:0        ब्राह्मणको:102
chhatraharupratiko छात्राहरूप्रतिको:0   छात्रहरूप्रतिको:0
kutuhaltako        कुतूहलताको:0         कुतुहल्ताको:0
rukho              रूखो:31              रुखो:193
chijharu           चीजहरू:230           चिजहरू:55
```

`छात्रा + हरू + प्रति + को` is four morphemes deep and gets a hard zero from a
word-unigram table. **This is W2's morphological prior, validated concretely rather
than by analogy to Finnish.**

### What this does to the sequencing

W1 and W2 now carry more of the matra class than the plan assumed, and W4 narrows
to its irreducible core — the 26.5% where both readings are OOV and only shape can
decide. W4 stays, but it moves *after* W2 and its expected contribution is smaller
and better understood. W1 remains first.

Reproduce: `./target/release/analyze_errors` (defaults are now the benchmark config).
Baseline cross-check, same run: `evaluate_model` AK-Freq top1 80.98 / top50 94.40 —
matches the documented numbers exactly, so the harness is trustworthy.

## W1 — Jackknifed large-scale discriminative reranking

**The defect.** The initial discriminative softmax was trained on the
**9,155-case valid split** — the only leakage-free k-best lists available, since
the EM model saw all of train. Cold-start full-softmax "diverged from the
heuristic on the native bucket", so the trained model was blended down to
`GAMMA = 0.3` (`src/core/reranker_weights.rs`). A 52-parameter model on 9k
examples, used at 30% strength, recovered **+0.71** of a 13.00-point ranking gap.
That is not the model family failing; it is a sample-size failure.

**The fix — large-scale discriminative training directly in Rust**
using the streaming, memory-safe trainer `src/bin/train_reranker.rs`.
Split `train_devanagari.jsonl` into chunks; train log-linear softmax
over the entire 3.59M pair dataset without leaking or exhausting RAM.

Cost: multi-pass streaming training over 3.59M pairs at beam 256. This
is the overnight run the compute budget was approved for.

**Feature space.** 29 dense features (emission, LM, length, frequency, morphology)
and **sparse lexicalized hash templates** — the class of feature that only becomes
trainable at this data scale:

- final-akshara id; first-akshara id; (final akshara × final roman char)
- (matra id × preceding consonant id) — the direct matra-confusable discriminator
- akshara-count − roman-length delta, bucketed
- per-akshara "this chunk is the emission argmax for this akshara" count
- morph-suffix id × roman tail (from W2's segmentation)
- candidate-source indicators (W3)

Hash into a fixed $2^{20}$ table; ship as `i8` quantized (1 MB) alongside the
dense weights in `src/core/reranker_weights.rs` and `src/core/reranker_weights_sparse.bin`.

**Objective, unchanged in form** — the per-case conditional likelihood already
implemented, which is a log-linear CRF over the k-best list:

$$
\mathcal{L} = \sum_{i} \log \frac{\exp(w \cdot \phi(R_i, D_i^{\text{gold}}))}{\sum_{D \in K_{50}(R_i)} \exp(w \cdot \phi(R_i, D))} - \frac{\lambda}{2}\|w\|^2
$$

Retune $\gamma$ on valid; the prediction is that $\gamma \to 1$ becomes optimal
once the model is trained on millions of cases. Keep the existing 5-fold-CV-on-
valid + single-shot-test protocol and the `src/bin/parity_v2.rs` gate (5/5).

**If softmax underperforms**, swap the objective to k-best MIRA with
Levenshtein loss — the DirecTL+ formulation
([Jiampojamarn et al.](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/df183a6aaae3e088d554cbe894dd945f71f47c3d)), the
non-neural SOTA for exactly this string-transduction task at the NEWS shared
tasks. Same features, same dumps, different update rule.

**Expected: +3–4.** Charniak & Johnson closed ~30% of the parsing oracle gap by
reranking; 30% of 13.00 = 3.9. → **~85%**

W1 is a *list-level* conditional model; the uncommitted S3 CRF is an *edge-level*
one. They are complementary, not competing — see the S3 decision below.

## W2 — A prior that is defined everywhere (kill the OOV cliff)

**The defect.** The word prior reaches the model as three features: `log1p(freq)`,
`freq_rank_pct`, `in_vocab` (`src/core/reranker.rs`). For any word
outside the 470k vocabulary — which is *exactly* the NE bucket where the ranking
gap is ~34 points — these are $0$, $1.0$, $0$. The prior is a step function with
no gradient in the region where it is needed most. `ln(1+f)` is also not a
calibrated probability.

**The fix.** Replace with a properly interpolated word model, exposed as separate
features so the interpolation weights are *learned by W1*, never hand-set:

$$
P(w) \;=\; \lambda_1 P_{\mathrm{KN}}^{\mathrm{uni}}(w) \;+\; \lambda_2 P_{\mathrm{morph}}(w) \;+\; \lambda_3 P_{\mathrm{aks}}^{\mathrm{corpus}}(w)
$$

1. **$P_{\mathrm{KN}}^{\mathrm{uni}}$** — absolute discounting on token counts
   rather than `ln(1+f)`, so the ≥3 prune cliff in
   `src/bin/build_wordfreq_text.rs:61` stops being a hard zero.

2. **$P_{\mathrm{morph}}$** — Nepali is agglutinative (घर+मा+को); an unseen
   inflection of a frequent stem currently gets the same zero as junk. Segment the
   470k vocab with an MDL/Morfessor-Baseline objective (~200 lines, no
   dependency — minimize $\text{codelength}(\text{lexicon}) + \text{codelength}(\text{corpus} \mid \text{lexicon})$):

$$
P_{\mathrm{morph}}(w) = \max_{w = s \cdot t} P_{\text{stem}}(s)\, P_{\text{suffix}}(t \mid \text{class}(s))
$$

   Morph-based LMs are the standard OOV remedy for morphologically rich languages
   ([Morfessor EM+Prune](https://aclanthology.org/2020.lrec-1.486.pdf); morph-based
   ASR across Finnish/Estonian/Turkish).

3. **$P_{\mathrm{aks}}^{\mathrm{corpus}}$** — the token-weighted akshara KN LM from
   running text. `src/bin/build_lm_from_text.rs` already builds this. It was
   measured negative in 2026-09-03 (73.01% alone, 75.05% mixed) — **but note what
   that table also shows: top-5 rose 90.23 → 91.65 while top-1 fell.** It improves
   coverage and hurts hand-tuned ranking. That is a fatal result for a *decoder LM
   replacement* and a promising one for a *reranker feature* and a *candidate
   source* (W3). Do not swap the decoder LM again; add the score as a feature.

New artifacts: morph tables ≈ 2 MB, corpus akshara LM ≈ 8 MB (or feature-only,
computed offline into the dumps for the first experiment — zero shipping cost
until it proves out).

**Expected: +1–2**, concentrated in the NE and rare-inflection buckets that
frequency cannot reach.

## W3 — Raise the oracle by candidate union, not by a better single decoder

**The defect.** 5.60 points are unreachable because gold never enters the k=50
list. S2 tried to fix this with exact word-trie *scores as a reranker feature* and
measured null (61.01 → 60.47) — correctly, since a score cannot conjure a
candidate the beam never produced. The trie's real value is as a **candidate
source**, which was never tested.

**The fix — system combination over the union of k-best lists** (standard MT/ASR
practice; union n-best strictly cannot lower the oracle). Merge candidates from:

- (a) the shipped decoder (`decode_detailed`, beam 256, k=50);
- (b) the same decoder with the corpus-text akshara LM (W2.3) — measured +1.4 at
  top-5, i.e. it generates words the shipped LM does not;
- (c) `decode_in_words` — the word-trie-constrained beam at
  `src/core/decoder.rs:305-406`, **already implemented and currently only reachable
  from `evaluate_model --intersect`**; it enumerates only real vocabulary words;
- (d) a morph-constrained decode: stem-trie ∘ suffix-FSA from W2.2, which generates
  *unseen but well-formed* inflections that neither (a) nor (c) can;
- (e) **the S3 CRF decode** — `evaluate_model --crf`, already wired. Its edge weights
  are discriminatively estimated rather than EM-estimated, so it makes *systematically
  different* candidates from (a). This is S3's home (see below).

Rerank the union with W1's model, adding one indicator feature per source. Note
(c) does not currently keep `emit`/`lm` separate (line 365) — split them so union
candidates carry the same feature vector as native ones.

**Expected: oracle 94.40 → 96–97, top-1 +1–2.** Latency cost is 2–4× decode;
budget it back with beam 128, which the log records as −0.29 points.

## W4 — Matra restoration as a factored second stage (the dominant class)

**The idea.** Factor the target into skeleton and vowel assignment,
$D = (C, V)$ with $C = \mathrm{strip\_matras}(D)$:

$$
P(D \mid R) \;=\; \underbrace{P(C \mid R)}_{\approx\,91\%\ \text{accurate today}} \cdot \underbrace{P(V \mid C, R)}_{\text{currently only 11 dense count features}}
$$

The current stack conflates these. The whole model — 3.59M pairs of EM, a KN
akshara LM, a 52-feature reranker — is spent on a factor that is already ~91%
right, while the factor carrying most of the residual error mass is represented by
per-matra *count* features (`reranker_v2.rs:30-41`) that cannot see *which* slot
got which matra.

**Why this is tractable: the supervision is free and enormous.** For every one of
the 86.1M corpus tokens, $C$ is a deterministic function of the token and $R$ is
generated by the existing backward romanizer (`src/bin/romanize.rs`, plus its
probability-weighted variants). That is a supervised slot-labelling corpus **24×
larger than the entire annotation set**, drawn from the true token distribution
rather than the annotation type distribution — and it requires no new data
collection.

**Model.** Per-slot classifier over matra labels (the 10 matras + none + nasal
marks), features: previous/next consonant id, slot index and slot-count, the roman
characters aligned to that slot, the roman vowel character, word-final vs internal,
and the morph-suffix id from W2. Second-order CRF or averaged perceptron —
identical in form to diacritic restoration for Arabic and Vietnamese, where
second-order CRFs reach **93–94% term accuracy**
([survey](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/328419851_A_Survey_of_Approaches_to_Diacritic_Restoration)).

**Two deployment modes, both cheap:**
- *Rescoring* — a feature $\log P(V \mid C, R)$ over the k=50 list, which
  discriminates precisely within a skeleton class (the matra-only confusion set)
  where every other feature is nearly constant;
- *Generation* — enumerate the top matra assignments for the top skeletons and
  add them to W3's union, which also lifts the oracle.

**Expected: +3–5.** Perfect $V$ gives 91.1 by the arithmetic above; capturing
50–70% of that class is the realistic band, and it overlaps partly with W1.

## S3 decision — yes, re-scoped from "replacement decoder" to "candidate source"

**BLOCKED (2026-09-05): the S3 code is gone from the working tree.** `src/core/crf.rs`,
`src/bin/train_crf.rs` and the `--crf` / decoder / `mod.rs` modifications were present
at the start of this session and are no longer on disk; `data/crf_model.bin` (88 MB) is
gone too. Nothing was ever committed, so git cannot restore it, and there is no stash.
The only survivor is the compiled `target/release/train_crf` binary, which can still
regenerate a model — but evaluating it needs the `--crf` decoder hook rewritten
(~40 lines: `CrfModel`, `edge_parts`, the `DecoderConfig` field, the eval flag).
The step-1 measurement below is therefore deferred until the code is restored or rebuilt.

**Keep it.** S3 is the only lever in this program that touches *generation*; W1, W2
and W4 are all ranking-side. The roadmap is right that the 94.40 oracle will not move
from the ranking stage. But three properties of the code as written make
"swap the decoder to the CRF" the wrong deployment:

- **Size**: `data/crf_model.bin` is 88 MB of sparse HashMap weights, against the
  32 MB `translit_model.bin` it would replace and a 21 MB → 9.1 MB brotli WASM budget.
- **A silent regression**: `CrfModel::edge_parts(st.prev, ...)` is **first-order**, so
  the CRF path drops the trigram fluency the EM path uses whenever `prev2` is available.
  Any head-to-head is confounded by that alone.
- **Objective mismatch**: it maximizes $\log P(\text{gold akshara path} \mid R)$ —
  path probability, not top-1 list accuracy. W1 optimizes the metric directly.
  Training also covered only 200k of 3.59M pairs for 2 epochs.

**Plan, in order:**

1. **Measure what already exists — ~1 hour, do this in W0.** `train_crf` and
   `evaluate_model --crf` are built and `crf_model.bin` is trained; the experiment has
   earned a number and never been given one. Log AK-Freq top-1 *and* the top-50 oracle;
   the oracle is the number that decides its fate, not top-1.
2. **If the CRF oracle beats 94.40** — even with top-1 below baseline — it is generating
   candidates the EM decoder cannot, which is exactly what the 5.60-point generation gap
   needs. Wire it in as union source (e) in W3 with a source-indicator feature, and let
   W1's reranker decide how far to trust it. No decoder swap, no shipping problem: the
   union runs offline for the benchmark, and only the *candidates* it contributes need
   to reach the engine.
3. **Then repair it properly**, in this order (each is independently measurable):
   restore trigram context in `edge_parts`; train on the full 3.59M pairs for more
   epochs (the sampling cap exists because full-lattice forward-backward allocates a
   `HashMap` per position — worth profiling before scaling); prune/quantize weights to
   fit budget only if it earns a shipping slot.
4. **If the CRF oracle does not beat 94.40 after the trigram repair**, park it with the
   number written up. It will have been falsified honestly, which is worth more than
   leaving it open in the roadmap indefinitely.

Also clean up before any of this: `src/core/crf.rs` has dead statements (`let _ = cid`,
`let _ = av`, an `alpha[0].insert` immediately followed by `clear`), three
`AKSHAR_CRF_DEBUG` blocks from chasing degenerate lattices, and no tests. The
`DecoderConfig` losing `Copy` also forced `evaluate_model.rs` to hardcode
`max_emission_weight: 8.0` instead of `..Default::default()` — restore that.

## W5 — Context, for the number users actually feel

Tracked, not the driver. The E6 layer is a greedy per-word additive boost
(`bigram_boost · ln(1+f)`, `src/core/engine.rs:411-431`). Replace with **word-lattice
Viterbi over the sentence** — defer commitment, decode
$\arg\max_{w_1..w_n} \sum_i [\log P(w_i \mid R_i) + \log P(w_i \mid w_{i-1})]$ across
the sentence rather than fixing each word greedily. This is what production Pinyin
and the Google transliteration IMEs do; Kirov et al. (2024) measure **3.3% absolute
/ 18.6% relative WER reduction** from preceding-word context
([Computational Linguistics 50(2)](https://aclanthology.org/2024.cl-2.2/)) — roughly
10× the +0.31–0.40 the greedy version delivers. Measured with the existing
`src/bin/evaluate_context.rs` A/B lockstep harness.

## Sequencing and honest ceiling

| Step | Effort | Δ AK-Freq | Running |
|---|---|---|---|
| W0 measurement gate | hours | — | 81.40 |
| W1 jackknifed reranking | 1 overnight + days | +3–4 | ~85 |
| W2 everywhere-defined prior | days | +1–2 | ~86–87 |
| W3 candidate union (incl. S3 as a source) | days | +1–2 | ~87–89 |
| W4 matra restoration | ~1 week | +3–5 (overlapping) | **89–92** |
| W5 sentence lattice | days | benchmark-invisible | sentence acc → ~91 |

The bands overlap — W1's lexicalized matra×consonant features and W4's slot model
attack the same errors — so the realistic landing zone is **88–91 on the benchmark**,
with >90 depending on W4 and on W0's collision bound permitting it. W5 independently
takes the real-typing number past 90. If W0 shows the isolated-word collision bound
is below ~92, that finding goes in the docs and the headline claim becomes the
context-aware number, with the benchmark reported honestly alongside.
